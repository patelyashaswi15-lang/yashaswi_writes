---
title: 'The line between product and growth stopped existing'
description: 'Every 2026 trend report says PM and marketing roles are merging. Three years of doing both at once says that split was never really there.'
pubDate: 'Sep 19 2026'
---

Every trend report this year says the same thing: product managers are picking up growth work, and growth marketers are picking up product work. Reading through a stack of these predictions for 2026, I kept waiting for the part that surprised me. It never came, because I've been doing both jobs at once since 2022.

At Elixir Creations I wrote user stories in the morning and ran Meta ad budgets in the afternoon. Nobody called it "product-led growth." It was just the job, because the company was five people and there was no one else to hand either half to. I scaled BookMyPainting to ₹30L in revenue by fixing SEO and building a 12-agent logistics network, then turned around and onboarded 15,000+ students to SpeakinEnglishClub through Meta ads I built myself, ₹150 CPA, tested down to the creative.

At American Air Filters, a company that's been around for a hundred years, the split was supposed to be real. Product on one side, marketing on the other. It wasn't. I was building Power BI dashboards to track campaign performance and directing Figma briefs for the same product's positioning in the same week. The 40% traffic growth I drove there came from treating SEO, content, and paid channels as one lever, not three separate teams' problems.

So when I read that "the PM role is splitting," my honest reaction is: splitting from what? I'm not convinced the clean separation ever existed outside of large companies with enough headcount to pretend it did. What's actually happening is that smaller teams, and now apparently bigger ones too, are admitting out loud what was already true: someone has to own the outcome, and job titles don't change what the work requires.

I don't have a tidy answer for whether this is good for product management as a discipline, or whether it just means PMs are quietly absorbing marketing's headcount cuts. Probably both. But if you're hiring a PM in 2026 and they've never touched a CAC number or run an ad account themselves, I'd ask why.
